thematics
design system, v0.1

A library of themes — read carefully, linked deliberately.

Thematics organizes knowledge by theme, not by folder. The system is light-first, set in Source Serif and IBM Plex, and uses a single hyperlink blue so the reader's eye always knows where the connections live.

Foundations

Surfaces, ink, and the single shade of blue that does all the link-work in the product.

Surfaces.Paper white for the reader; a faint warm off-white for cards and rails; two deeper neutrals for wells and hover.
Ink.Three levels — body, metadata, and tertiary. Each is contrast-checked on paper white.
Hyperlink blue.Reserved for the moments a reader's about to follow a connection or commit a change. It never fills chrome.
Subject tags.Five hues for theme-coding. Pick by subject, not by hierarchy — they all read at the same weight.

Typography

Source Serif 4 reads the prose. IBM Plex Sans runs the chrome. IBM Plex Mono is reserved for things that are literally code — slugs, DOIs, identifiers.

Display & headings.Source Serif, optical-sized, at 56 / 40 / 28 / 22.
Prose.The reading column lives at 18 over 1.6 in serif. This is the only place we let the type take over.
UI sans.Plex Sans at 14 / 1.5 for everything that isn't reading.
Mono — sparingly.For slugs, DOIs, raw identifiers. Citations get the source's own mark and name, not a mono-numeric label.

Brand

A lowercase serif wordmark and an asterism (⁂) — the centuries-old typographic glyph for a section break. We've co-opted it to mean theme.

Wordmark & mark.Source Serif 4 at 600 weight, with the asterism leading.
The asterism, in context.It does double duty as section break inside a long entry. Reserved for these two roles only — never decorative.

Shape & elevation

Depth comes from hairlines and surface tint. Shadows appear only when an element genuinely rises off the page.

Radii.4 / 6 / 8 / 12 / 16 / pill. Cards at 12, surfaces at 16, chips full-round.
Shadows.Three depths, used only on popovers, hover-elevated cards, and modals.

Components

The vocabulary the reading and authoring surfaces share. Every interaction defaults to quiet; primary blue only lights up where the reader is about to follow a link or commit a change.

Buttons.Primary, secondary, danger, link, icon. No ghost variant — quiet actions become text links.
Inputs, selects, toggles.Hairline borders by default; a 3px primary ring on focus.
Theme & filter chips.The only widespread use of color outside primary — and only on chips, never on chrome.
Search & command menu.Opens to a grouped result list, keyboard-first.
Entry & theme cards.The two formats the explorer uses — both lead with the title; metadata reads as a sentence.
Crossref hover preview.The signature interaction — a link expands inline, with the destination's chip, title, lede, and a way in.

Inline primitives

Small visual figures — charts, diagrams, comparison cards, sliders — that drop inline into an entry's reading column or a chat response. Same column width, same hairlines, same serif captions. They earn their place when an idea is faster to see than to read. Read the full spec →

Metric.One number with its label. Numbers set in Source Serif so the figure feels like part of the entry, not a dashboard cell.
Comparison cards.Three options for a real choice. The recommended option uses a hairline blue border and an inline label — never a floating badge.
Data record.One bounded object — a person, a citation, an entry's metadata. Monogram tiles in serif, never circular.
Slider with live readout.Drives a one-sentence summary in serif. Drag the year and read what the Bauhaus looked like then.
Flowchart.Sequential steps, one branching decision. The decision node carries a soft amber tint; arrows route in L-shapes.
Structural.Things inside other things. One frame, hairline dividers, colour-coded only on the column labels — the way a printed table of contents would mark its sections.
Illustrative.A spatial metaphor for an abstract idea. The radial violet field is the system's one permitted gradient — only for continuous physical metaphors.
Line chart.Three series, three ramp hues — pre-built Chart.js with our Plex Sans defaults. Dashed segment marks the dissolution years.
Bar chart.Stacked horizontal across seven themes. Hatch on the middle series so colour isn't the only encoding — the chart parses in greyscale.
Choropleth.Real topology, ocean ramp. State borders drawn in paper white so the divisions read without competing with the data.

Voice

Thematics writes like a patient editor — not a marketer, not an assistant. Every empty state explains what the surface is for. Every error tells the reader what to do next.

Curious, careful, generous.

We name what's true — number of entries, last edit, who contributed — only when the reader needs it. Never as a row of pills under a title.

A theme is not a folder. It is a chosen vantage from which a constellation of entries reads coherently. — README, §1
Do — sentences, in plain serif
  • "No entries in this theme yet. Add the first one."
  • "Saved two minutes ago."
  • "Linked from fourteen entries."
  • "An eight-minute read."
Don't — metadata slop, marketing puff
  • "You don't have any documents yet 🚀"
  • "LAST EDITED · 14 BACKLINKS · ~8 MIN READ"
  • "Successfully saved your changes ✨"
  • "Estimated read time: 8 minutes"

Anti-patterns

Things the system explicitly forbids. These are the visual tells of generic, design-system-by-the-numbers work — they have no place in Thematics.

Metadata slop

Chains of mono numbers strung under a title to look authoritative. If a number is worth surfacing, it earns a sentence.

Uppercase-mono overlines

Letter-spaced caps used as labels. Rigidity without information. If a section needs a label, use its heading.

Ghost buttons

Transparent buttons read as not-buttons. Quiet actions become text links instead.

Specimen-card chrome

The rounded-top header strip with title-left, tag-right, and a separator. Design-system clip-art. Specimens sit in flow with a sentence-case caption.

Hero stat strips

Four-column rows of mono numerals under a hero. Same family as metadata slop.

Emoji in chrome

The em-dash, the asterism, and Lucide icons are the entire pictographic vocabulary.

A reading surface, in full

The reader is the product's centerpiece — a 720-pixel column flanked by an outline on the left and backlinks on the right. Everything else is exhaust.

The Bauhaus pedagogy

A school built on the premise that a year of seeing must precede a single act of making.

The Vorkurs — Bauhaus shorthand for the preliminary course — arrived in 1919 not as a curriculum but as a refusal. Johannes Itten insisted that a student's prior habits, however well-meant, were the first obstacle to the craft they came to learn. A year of unlearning preceded any workshop assignment.

By 1923 the experiment had drifted. László Moholy-Nagy, newly arrived from Berlin, pulled the foundation course decisively toward industrial form. The break with Itten was sharp; the curriculum followed within a single term.

⁂

What survived was the underlying conviction: that a school of design must begin with seeing, not making. The form of the course changed; the premise traveled — to Chicago, to Ulm, to Black Mountain — and shaped the next half-century of design pedagogy.

Wingler, The Bauhaus: Weimar, Dessau, Berlin, Chicago. MIT Press, 1969, pages 33–47.
